Stocks & Flows

Three hands-on tools for playing with the systems behind Chapter 10. Turn the knobs, run the clock, and watch how stocks rise, fall, and feed back on themselves.

Companion to Ch. 10 · Systems and Cycles — Environmental Science (W. W. Norton, 2023)

Tool 01 — One Stock, Two Flows

The faucet & the drain

The simplest system: one stock with an inflow and an outflow. Pick a real example, move the two sliders, and press Run. Watch the tank fill or drain — and notice the chart's slope is the net flow.

Tool 02 — Many Stocks, Connected

The carbon cycle as a system of reservoirs

Real systems chain stocks together — an outflow from one is an inflow to another, so carbon is conserved. Here are Earth's four great carbon reservoirs (real sizes, in gigatonnes of carbon). Adjust the flows below and run the clock to see where the carbon goes.

Atmospheric CO₂ over time

The dashed lines mark pre-industrial CO₂ (280 ppm) and the 350 ppm level often cited as a safe ceiling. Outflows from fossil and land become inflows to the atmosphere — total carbon never changes. (In this simplified model the sink rates stay fixed; on the real Earth they rise and fall with CO₂ levels.)

Tool 03 — When Flows Depend on the Stock

Feedback loops you can run

A loop forms when a flow is wired back to the stock that drives it. Pick a loop type, set its strength, and run it — then watch the shape of the curve each loop produces. The live diagram shows which loop is steering the system right now.

Data sources & further reading

Every quantitative value in these tools comes from the sources below.

  1. Canadell, J. G., et al. (2021). Global Carbon and Other Biogeochemical Cycles and Feedbacks. In IPCC AR6, WG1, Ch. 5 (Fig. 5.12). Reservoir sizes: atmosphere ≈ 875 GtC, ocean (dissolved inorganic carbon) ≈ 38,000 GtC, land plants + soils ≈ 2,150 GtC, recoverable fossil reserves ≈ 1,000 GtC. ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/chapter-5
  2. Friedlingstein, P., et al. (2025). Global Carbon Budget 2024. Earth System Science Data, 17, 965–1039. Fossil-fuel CO₂ ≈ 9.6–10.1 GtC yr⁻¹; land-use change ≈ 1.1–1.6; ocean sink ≈ 2.5–2.9; land sink ≈ 3.1–3.4; atmospheric growth ≈ 5 GtC yr⁻¹. essd.copernicus.org/articles/17/965/2025
  3. Lan, X., Tans, P., & Thoning, K. W. — NOAA Global Monitoring Laboratory (2024). Trends in Atmospheric Carbon Dioxide. 422 ppm (2024); pre-industrial ≈ 278 ppm; 1 ppm ≈ 2.124 GtC. gml.noaa.gov/ccgg/trends
  4. Hansen, J., et al. (2008). Target Atmospheric CO₂: Where Should Humanity Aim? The Open Atmospheric Science Journal, 2, 217–231. Source of the 350 ppm reference level. doi.org/10.2174/1874282300802010217
  5. Severinghaus, J. P., Broecker, W. S., Dempster, W. F., MacCallum, T., & Wahlen, M. (1994). Oxygen Loss in Biosphere 2. Eos, 75(3), 33–37. O₂ fell from 20.9% toward ~14% over the first 16 months (1991–93). doi.org/10.1029/94EO00285
  6. Verhulst, P.-F. (1838). Notice sur la loi que la population suit dans son accroissement. Correspondance Mathématique et Physique, 10, 113–121. Origin of the logistic (S-curve) growth model used in the feedback tool.
  7. Meadows, D. H. (2008). Thinking in Systems: A Primer. Chelsea Green Publishing. Source of the stock-and-flow and feedback-loop framing throughout.
  8. Houtman, A., Karr, S., & Interlandi, J. (2023). Environmental Science: Are We Harming Earth's Life-Support System? (Ch. 10). W. W. Norton & Company. Source chapter for the Biosphere 2 case and carbon-cycle narrative.
